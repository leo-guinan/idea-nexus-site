# Humanpower

AI is not the unit of value.

People are.

The question is whether a system increases what people can do together without making their judgment, relationships, or responsibility disappear.

## What humanpower means here

Humanpower is the productive capability of a human network: the range of action available to its people, the quality of their decisions, and the speed and integrity with which they can coordinate.

It is not a motivational slogan. It is a systems question.

A capable network can:

- notice what matters before it becomes obvious;
- bring the right context to the right decision;
- coordinate across roles without flattening differences;
- preserve trust, provenance, and dissent;
- learn from outcomes instead of repeating folklore;
- act with enough agency to change the conditions it is observing.

## How we measure it

There is no single humanpower score. A single score would be a convenient way to hide the argument.

We measure a baseline across six dimensions:

### Range of action

What can this network do now that it could not do before? Which decisions, interventions, or experiments became possible?

### Decision quality

Are decisions better supported by relevant context, alternative views, and explicit uncertainty? Are errors detected earlier?

### Coordination latency

How long does it take for a meaningful signal to reach the person who can act on it? Where does work stall between knowing and doing?

### Context retention

Does important knowledge survive handoffs, staff changes, and time? Can a person reconstruct why a decision was made?

### Trust and agency

Can people inspect the path from source to recommendation? Can they disagree, correct the system, and remain responsible for the outcome?

### Learning rate

Does the network change its behavior when evidence changes? Are outcomes recorded well enough to improve the next attempt?

## The smallest credible test

We do not begin by claiming that an AI system will make a network more capable.

We map one real workflow, choose one bottleneck, establish a baseline, and run the smallest intervention that could falsify the thesis. The intervention may be AI. It may be process redesign, a clearer decision boundary, better memory, or nothing at all.

The receipt is not a demo. It is a before-and-after account of what changed, for whom, at what cost, and with which risks.
