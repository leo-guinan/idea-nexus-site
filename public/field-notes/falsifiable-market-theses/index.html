Falsifiable market theses

A market thesis is not a mood, a narrative, or a list of companies that already went up.

It is a claim about a mechanism that should produce observable consequences within a stated window.

A useful thesis contains

  • the mechanism;
  • the network that carries it;
  • the evidence already visible;
  • the leading indicators;
  • the time window;
  • the conditions that would falsify it;
  • the receipt that will resolve it.

Why the network matters

The visible event is often late. Information travels through people, institutions, communities, vendors, regulators, and capital before it becomes a consensus narrative.

Network analysis does not make the future certain. It helps identify where the narrative is forming, who can move it, and which observation would tell us we were wrong.

The discipline

Publish the conviction before the outcome. Preserve the miss. Do not quietly turn a failed prediction into a lesson about why it was secretly right.
