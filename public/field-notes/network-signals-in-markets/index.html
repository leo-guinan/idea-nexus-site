FIELD NOTE

Network signals in markets

A market event is often the visible end of a longer information journey.

Before a narrative becomes consensus, it moves through relationships: operators, customers, suppliers, regulators, communities, analysts, and capital. The network does not guarantee that a thesis is right. It can show where attention and coordination are accumulating.

What to look for

  • repeated information crossing otherwise separate groups;
  • unusually fast movement from claim to action;
  • trusted nodes changing language or behavior;
  • new coordination infrastructure appearing around an old problem;
  • disagreement that persists despite shared evidence.

What would falsify it

A signal is not useful merely because it can be narrated afterward. The thesis needs a time window and an observable consequence. If the expected coordination, adoption, or repricing does not occur, the thesis is wrong or incomplete.
