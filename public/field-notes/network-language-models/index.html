Network language models

A language model usually sees a sequence. An organization lives in a network.

The difference matters. A useful system must know not only what was said, but who knows it, who trusts it, which decision it informs, what remains unresolved, and how it got here.

The intervention

A network language model is not simply a larger model or a smarter chatbot. It is a system that preserves relationships around information:

  • people and roles;
  • decisions and owners;
  • sources and provenance;
  • questions and unresolved edges;
  • actions and outcomes.

The point is not to automate every conversation. The point is to make coordination less lossy.

The test

Does network context improve the quality and speed of a decision without making human judgment invisible?

The smallest credible test is a bounded workflow with a known decision, a small group of users, and a receipt showing what changed.

Boundary

If the network is poorly defined, the data is untrusted, or the decision rights are unclear, adding a model may only make confusion move faster.
